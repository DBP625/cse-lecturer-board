What to Read — Written Exam & Viva

I opened all eight semesters. Your notes are unusually well-organized — most subjects already hold a Term-Final Q&A, a Suggestions sheet, and question banks spanning a decade of batches. This maps each subject to exactly which of your own files to open, in order — no re-reading textbooks cover to cover.

The method — why your Drive beats any textbook

Private-uni written tests reward recall of core courses, not depth. Your folders are already sorted by exam-value — so read them in that order, per subject:

1
Term-Final Q&A + Suggestions first. These compress years of real questions into the answers that actually come up. You have them for OS, TOC, Data Com, Microprocessor, SAD, SWE. e.g. “O.S. - Term Final-Q&A.pdf”, “Suggesstions.pdf”
2
Section-wise / chapter-wise question banks next. They reveal the exam’s own weighting — where the marks concentrate. e.g. “C.N. Questions (sectionwise).pdf”, “S.W.E. (chapterwise).pdf”
3
Multi-batch question PDFs to spot repeats. The same 20–30 questions recur across batches; mark the ones that appear 3+ times. e.g. “Questions (22-18,16-14,12,11,09,08).pdf”
4
Notes folder before Slides. Notes are already exam-distilled; slides move at teaching pace. Open Slides only for a topic the notes leave fuzzy.

Written test shape

Tier 1 = the demo overlap

5 subjects

Data Structures, Algorithms, DBMS, OS, Networks — highest written yield and your demo-class topics come from here. Spend most hours in this tier.

Viva ≠ written

AI + ML

The board probes your specialization. AI/ML files (incl. “Top 50 ML Interview Q&A”) plus your own specialization carry the viva — even though they’re low-priority for the written test.

Reading order — if you have two weeks

stack it so the demo-overlap subjects get your freshest hours
Wave 1days 1–6

Data Structures · Algorithms · DBMS · Operating Systems · Computer Networks · (OOP/Programming)

The Tier-1 five + programming. Each: Q&A → section-wise bank → notes. These are also your three demo topics (BST, 3NF, TCP handshake), so teaching-sheet them as you go.

Wave 2days 7–11

Discrete Math · DLD · Theory of Computing · Computer Architecture · Software Engineering

Tier 2. SWE and TOC are pure Q&A-skim (huge answer banks, little derivation) — fast marks. DLD/CA need the worked problems.

Wave 3days 12–14

Data Communication · SAD · Microprocessors → then AI · Machine Learning

Skim the Term-Final Q&A for the first three. Then pivot to viva: AI + ML + your specialization. Read “Top 50 ML Interview Q&A” slowly — it doubles as viva rehearsal.

Don’t spend written-exam hours here: Chemistry, Physics, Math (calculus/vector), ME Drawing, Economics, Sociology, Accounting, Engineering Management, DSP, Electrical Drives. They’re in your Drive but never on a CSE lecturer test. Discrete Math is the one “math” that counts — it’s CS.
