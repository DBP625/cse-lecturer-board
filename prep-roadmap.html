Step 4 — viva pack

what the board across the table will actually ask

The 2-minute research pitch

Memorize the spine, not a script:

Summarize your final-year research in four beats — the problem it tackled, your approach, one headline result, and where you’d take it next. Keep it near two minutes.

Then stop talking. Let them ask.

“Which courses can you take?”

From your own transcript of coverage — lead with:

Structured ProgrammingOOPData StructuresAlgorithmsDBMSAI / ML Operating SystemsNetworksDiscrete MathTheory of ComputingDLD+ every sessional/lab

Say yes to labs unprompted — departments are chronically short on sessional coverage and it signals a team player.

The other five questions

  • Why teaching over industry? Answer with evidence from your own choices — the projects you picked and why.
  • Research plan? Describe how you’d extend your own research direction; name a venue you’d target.
  • M.Sc. plans? “Enrolling part-time at CUET in the coming session” — you have the circulars; boards want to hear the ladder plan.
  • Salary expectation? Name your tier’s band from the notice board — upper half if your CGPA + publication back it.
  • Availability? Immediate. Fresh graduate, defended 15 July — no notice period is a genuine advantage over employed candidates.

Before any interview call

  • Thesis paper submitted to a venue — “under review” transforms the publication answer.
  • Google Scholar + ORCID live, LinkedIn current.
  • Supervisor (and one more faculty referee) warned by email that calls may come.
  • Hard-copy file assembled per the board’s document checklist — certificates SSC→B.Sc., NID, photos, publication reprint.
  • One printed copy of the demo-class teach sheets riding in the same folder — walking in with them is its own signal.
